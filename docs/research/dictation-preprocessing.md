# Dictation preprocessing

## Primary-source references

- **Model input.** NVIDIA specifies 16 kHz mono audio for Parakeet TDT 0.6B v3. This is a requirement for the signal supplied to that model. A microphone can capture at a different rate if the input is resampled before inference. [NVIDIA Parakeet model card, Input](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3#input)
- **Capture guidance.** Google Cloud Speech-to-Text recommends placing the microphone close to the speaker, setting levels to prevent clipping, and disabling automatic gain control (AGC) and upstream denoising. For its API, Google prefers a 16 kHz source when feasible and recommends preserving the native sample rate for headerless audio recorded at another rate. These recommendations reflect the service’s own recognition pipeline. [Google Cloud Speech-to-Text best practices](https://docs.cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/docs/best-practices#audio_preprocessing)
- **Enhancement artifacts.** Iwamoto et al. tested a single-channel denoiser with an independently trained recognizer. In their experiments, reducing the artifacts introduced by the denoiser improved word error rate (WER) more than changing the amount of residual noise. Mixing some of the original signal back into the denoised signal lowered WER even as the signal-to-noise ratio fell. The result shows how denoising can hurt recognition in the tested pipeline. The effect on Parakeet depends on the preprocessing and recording conditions. [Iwamoto et al. (2022), §4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.06685#page=3)

## Benchmark

The installed SmallTalkAI CPU inference worker decoded mono 16 kHz PCM16 in one-shot mode with warm models. It used the same inference path and end padding as local dictation. The test bypassed the microphone, desktop, and text cleanup. It held the input and recognizer fixed across each comparison.

- **Main set.** 8 LibriSpeech test-clean clips, 8 test-other clips, 12 Egyptian Arabic FLEURS test clips, and 12 English dictation phrases generated by SmallTalkAI's Kokoro with three voices. Parakeet `tdt_ctc-110m-q8_0` decoded English and `lemura-arabic-asr-lite-q8_0` decoded Arabic. [LibriSpeech](https://www.openslr.org/12/) · [FLEURS](https://huggingface.co/datasets/google/fleurs) · [Kokoro](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M)
- **Additional English set.** Parakeet `tdt-0.6b-v3-q8_0` decoded 12 LibriSpeech clips from 12 speakers absent from the main set. The 110M model also decoded eight separate Kokoro prompts ending in words such as “drafts,” “boxes,” “buzz,” and “spice,” with four conditions per prompt.
- **Conditions.** Original speech with 0.5 seconds of padding at each edge, quiet speech at about −51 dBFS RMS, quiet speech with a ±0.11 sample offset, added broadband noise at +8 dB signal-to-noise ratio, low-frequency rumble, hard clipping, and an extra 1.5 seconds of silence at each edge. Every condition was applied to the same source clips in each set, except hard clipping was skipped for the additional 0.6B set. The ending set used original, quiet, broadband noise, and long-pause conditions.
- **Treatments.** The baseline used unity gain with no added processing. The alternatives were subtracting a recording-wide mean when its magnitude reached 0.01, a second-order 65 Hz Butterworth high-pass, boosting only quiet input (peak ≤0.08, RMS 0.001–0.008, up to 4× with a peak cap), applying mean removal followed by that boost, trimming low-energy edges while keeping 200 ms of context, and FFmpeg spectral denoising (`afftdn=nr=6:nf=-45:tn=1`).
- **Scoring.** Word error rate uses case and punctuation normalization and ignores Arabic diacritics and common spelling variants. The ending checks also require the final reference word to match exactly after normalization. Identical PCM within a clip and condition shared one decode. The 60 source clips produced 2,624 scored comparisons and 1,611 worker decodes.

| Recognizer | Condition | Treatment | Baseline WER | Treatment WER |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| Parakeet 110M | Original English | Light denoising | 2.23% | 2.90% |
| Parakeet 110M | English, +8 dB SNR | Light denoising | 7.81% | 6.92% |
| Lemura | Arabic, +8 dB SNR | Light denoising | 11.49% | 11.91% |
| Parakeet 110M | Quiet English | Bounded boost | 2.68% | 2.46% |
| Lemura | Quiet Arabic | Bounded boost | 7.66% | 7.23% |
| Parakeet 0.6B v3 | Quiet English, additional speakers | Bounded boost | 2.16% | 2.70% |
| Parakeet 110M | Quiet English with offset | Mean removal and boost | 2.90% | 2.46% |
| Parakeet 0.6B v3 | Quiet English with offset, additional speakers | Mean removal and boost | 2.70% | 2.70% |
| Lemura | Quiet Arabic | Edge trimming | 7.66% | 9.36% |
| Parakeet 110M | English with added rumble | 65 Hz high-pass | 2.68% | 2.46% |
| Parakeet 0.6B v3 | English with added rumble, additional speakers | 65 Hz high-pass | 2.70% | 2.70% |

The ending check matched **31 of 32** final words with the baseline and **28 of 32** with edge trimming. Boost, high-pass, and light denoising each matched 31 of 32. Edge trimming reduced median warm-worker time from 347 ms to 206 ms for Parakeet 110M with added edge silence, at the cost of three additional wrong final words in the separate ending check.

## Decision

Keep the current shared Linux and Windows dictation defaults. Conditional gain helped by one word each in the primary quiet English and Arabic sets, then added one error in the separate quiet English set. Denoising, high-pass filtering, and mean subtraction showed gains in individual conditions, with flat or adverse outcomes in others. Keep mean subtraction in the waveform display so steady microphone offset does not conceal speech activity. [Waveform research](dictation-waveform.md)

This is a small paired benchmark. The public recordings are read speech, the Kokoro input is synthetic, and the added noise and clipping are controlled simulations. The holdout is separate from the main benchmark selection. Model training overlap and performance on live microphones, other languages, and remote recognizers remain unverified.

## Microphone preview indicator

The shared preview now reports low level or clipping while recording. The detector waits for sustained low-level activity with variation and for repeated full-scale samples before warning. It meters the configured gain and uses mean-free RMS for loudness. Its result only changes the preview status.

A trial with 26 development clips and 12 additional LibriSpeech clips normalized speech to about −25 dBFS for normal level and −51 dBFS for quiet. Severe clipping was created by amplifying peaks to four times full scale and saturating. There were no alerts on 38 normal clips. The detector flagged clipping in all 38 saturated clips, quiet in 35 of 38 quiet clips, and quiet in 33 of 38 quiet clips with an injected steady offset. Digital silence, constant offset, steady hum, and steady low noise produced no alerts. The trial used public recordings and repository fixtures and modeled the detector rules. It did not measure the effect of warnings on people adjusting a physical microphone.

A separate Linux integration test fed normal, quiet, clipped, and silent audio to the capture service through an isolated PipeWire source and read each warning through the real Settings API. The quiet preview also returned the expected transcript from the local CPU model. Browser tests exercised both warning states and clearing the status.
